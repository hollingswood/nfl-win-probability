    How to read this

    Edge vs. factors. The factor bars explain how the model arrived at its own line, in points. They are not edges: Vegas sees most of the same things. The edge comes only from the gap between the model's line and Vegas's line, and the bet math deliberately shrinks that gap toward Vegas before calculating our chance. A game can have big factors and zero edge.

    Grades (A+ to C) start from the edge and subtract for warning signs: the model far from the market, a QB change, the wrong side of 3 or 7, or the line moving against us. They add for the line moving our way or the right side of a key number.

    Factor bars show how many points of predicted margin each input is worth and which team it favors. Home field is about 2 points, and near zero at neutral sites. Injuries count every non-QB player listed Out or Doubtful, weighted by their recent share of snaps; reports come out Wednesday to Friday, so early-week numbers assume everyone is healthy.

    Model total comes from a simple display-only model (each team's recent points scored and allowed, offensive and defensive efficiency, league scoring level, dome). On 2020–25 it missed the actual total by 10.6 points on average, against 10.3 for the closing Vegas total, so it is context, not an edge, and no track bets on it. AI-read news is pulled from public feeds by an AI reader and is not verified; it is not used by the model or any track.

    Travel, time zones, body clock, weather and Next Gen Stats were each tested as model inputs and did not improve out-of-sample accuracy, so they appear on the cards as context and are not part of the prediction. Live odds come from multiple sportsbooks when an Odds API key is set; "edge" is the expected return at the best available price using the model blended with the market (the same probability the paper bets use), and the backtest above shows those edges have not been profitable against closing lines.

    Vegas is a very strong benchmark. Matching it over a full season would be a good result; a large disagreement usually means the market knows something the model doesn't, like an injury announced after the data refresh.
