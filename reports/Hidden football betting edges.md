# Mine the price plumbing other bettors ignore

The edges most football bettors miss are not hidden predictors of the score. They sit in **how prices get made and handed down**. Market makers set a number, vendors and copycat books relay it with a lag, retail risk desks add their own margins, marketing teams subsidize some prices, and on exchanges retail flow overpays for longshots and parlays. This system's own record says the same thing from the other side. Every forecasting idea it has tried has failed against the close, while its one robust edge, college soft-book prices against Pinnacle, is a price-relay edge. Ranked by expected value × evidence × feasibility, the best next moves are mostly unglamorous. First, **open the six Arizona accounts the college shop track already prices but cannot bet**: in 2021-25 that would have more than doubled qualifying bets at a similar +2-4% CLV. Second, **poll college lines hourly on Sundays and Mondays**, when the system's college CLV is highest in nearly every season. Third, build an **event-triggered stale-price rule** that fires only after a fresh Pinnacle move. Fourth, a **boost and promo calculator** driven by the fair price. Fifth, a **favorite-side variant of the Kalshi maker track**, plus a measurement of Kalshi's liquidity rewards. More speculative leads follow: college availability reports as a new official data feed, a pre-registered test of whether totals lag rule changes, props-implied team totals and kicker props in wind. Almost none of the new ideas has measured football evidence behind it. They are mechanisms with cheap tests, and the report grades them that way. Three constraints apply to all of them. Kalshi's Arizona status could flip on a Ninth Circuit order. College player props are illegal at Arizona books. And because books limit winners by CLV, every live edge spends a finite budget of account life.

## The market's soft spots sit in how prices are made, not in what models forecast

The pros who still win at football describe themselves as traders, not handicappers. Gadoon "Spanky" Kyrollos says "we don't watch any games." His edge is "injuries, numbers, and line movement" filtered by software that took "seven, eight years to fine tune," and he **rarely bets the NFL because it is "not profitable"** for him ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/whats-the-difference-between-you-and-a-sharp-step-inside-the-mind-of-a-professional-bettor)). Rufus Peabody's group earns "over $1 million per year," yet NFL betting is "a small percentage" of it. He says "the same model I used to bet baseball then wouldn't make money now" ([TheLines](https://www.thelines.com/professional-sports-betting-journey-bettor-rufus-peabody-2022/)). Both originate where books lack data, in props, early college numbers and thin derivatives, and they take sharp-to-soft price gaps quickly. The system's own studies reached the same conclusion independently. Its model trails the close, situational factors decayed to zero, and the only passing rules are price rules.

What others miss is that each link in the price chain has its own kind of error. **Vendor clusters** share one feed. Arizona's Kambi-powered books are BetRivers, Desert Diamond and Bally Bet ([iGaming Future](https://igamingfuture.com/kambi-group-plc-signs-multi-year-partnership-with-desert-diamond-casinos-an-enterprise-of-the-tohono-oodham-nation/); [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/114299/2023-ballys-taps-kambi-sports-betting-app-restart/)), and one global trading desk prices them, with college football presumably low on its list. **Copier books** follow market makers. An industry consultancy says retail books "typically follow" Pinnacle and BetCRIS, with Circa leading college ([SCCG](https://sccgmanagement.com/areas-of-expertise/2024/1/10/market-maker-vs-retail-sportsbook-business-models-and-the-impact-of-price-discovery/)). **There is no sharp book for props.** FanDuel originates its own numbers, other books share a source, and Caesars "sometimes moves too aggressively" after early limit bets ([Establish The Run](https://establishtherun.com/understanding-the-current-ecosystem-of-nfl-player-props/)). **Marketing teams price boosts** against the book's own vigged line, and documented examples run 7-20% EV ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/education/bonus-hunting-for-existing-sportsbook-users)). On **exchanges**, takers lose about three times what makers lose, and longshots under 10¢ lose more than 60% ([Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). **Regulators** have just created new official data: in 2026 the Big Ten began publishing four availability reports a week ([Santa Fe New Mexican/AP](https://www.santafenewmexican.com/sports/big-ten-will-release-4-player-availability-reports-per-week-for-games-matching-conference-teams/article_b2453480-1e2a-5983-a8c1-9e651f7726cb.html)). None of these errors is visible to a bettor who only asks whether a team is better than its spread.

The second thing others miss is that **winning has a cost that compounds**. A Massachusetts regulator roundtable heard that CLV is the main limiting signal. A flagged sharp can be held to about 10% of a new account's limits, and every operator declined to attend ([Closing Line](https://closingline.substack.com/p/sportsbook-limits-get-a-closer-look)). Alan Denkenson says most people are limited "after about four bets," and that "laying 5 on a game that closes at 8" marks a sharp ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2019/6/5/18644504/sports-betting-bettors-sharps-kicked-out-spanky-william-hill-new-jersey)). The system's success metric is therefore the books' detection metric. **Edge should be counted per unit of account life, not per bet.** That is why promos, which look recreational, and exchanges, which do not limit, are worth more than their per-bet EV suggests.

Taxes enlarge that cost. From tax year 2026 only 90% of gambling losses are deductible ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nathangoldman/2026/01/13/gambling-tax-alert-new-law-cuts-loss-deductions-bettors-face-big-hit/)). My arithmetic for even-money bets with a 51% win rate shows what that does. Gross winnings are 0.51 of turnover and gross losses 0.49. Taxable income is 0.51 − 0.9 × 0.49 = **6.9% of turnover, against a true profit of 2%**. Low-margin, high-turnover strategies such as market making, reward farming, middling and hedged bonus conversion therefore owe tax on several times what they earn. High-EV, low-turnover bets (boosts, 4%+ shop bets) suffer least. Whether exchange contract gains or liquidity rewards count as gambling income is unresolved and needs a CPA.

The third thing is **statistical power**, which decides which ideas can be judged at all. In the college shop study, 113 spread bets at +3.8% CLV gave p ≈ 3e-10. That implies a per-bet CLV standard deviation of about 6.5% (my back-calculation from `output/research/cfb/round2.md`). Detecting +2% CLV at one-sided p < 0.05 then takes about **30 bets**. Detecting a +2% ROI on even-money results takes about **6,800**. A first-touchdown rule at +1500 averages a per-bet standard deviation near 3.9 units, so even a +15% ROI needs roughly **1,800 bets** just to reach significance. Ideas that can be graded on CLV against a sharp close are settled in weeks. Ideas that can only be graded on results take years. The ranking below weights this heavily.

## Book structure hands this system its cheapest new edge

### Six missing accounts would double the college shop's volume

The college shop track prices nine Arizona books (`cfb_shop_rules.json`), but `my_books.json` lists only DraftKings, FanDuel and theScore/ESPN Bet as bettable. The repo's own 2021-25 results put a number on that gap. With all Arizona books, the three EV≥2% rules found **1,873 bets, against 835 with the user's three**, at about the same CLV: spreads +1.7% vs +1.2%, totals +2.0% vs +2.6%, moneylines +2.9% vs +2.6% (`output/research/cfb/round2.md`). My breakdown of the 2,438 all-Arizona backtest bets (`cfb/shop_bets.parquet`, descriptive and post-hoc) shows where the extra volume comes from:

| Book | Bets | Mean CLV | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| FanDuel | 470 | +3.7% | held |
| BetRivers (Kambi) | 449 | +2.8% | not held |
| BetMGM | 427 | +2.5% | not held |
| Caesars | 294 | +0.5% | not held; weakest |
| DraftKings | 258 | +2.9% | held |
| Hard Rock | 219 | +3.3% | not held |
| Bally Bet (Kambi) | 170 | +3.3% | not held |
| theScore/ESPN Bet | 134 | +1.0% | held |

About **65% of the qualifying bets came from books the user does not hold**. BetRivers, BetMGM, Hard Rock and Bally Bet each carry CLV equal to or better than the user's books. Caesars adds little in college. Arizona had 14 licensed operators before the 2026 licensing round ([G3 Newswire](https://g3newswire.com/arizona-to-launch-tender-for-more-betting-licences/)). This is the highest expected value × evidence × feasibility item in the report. It needs no code, only account openings and a `my_books.json` edit, and its evidence is the system's own pre-registered, five-season result. Extra accounts also add account life: each new book is another budget of bets before limits. My rough estimate is about 200 extra college bets a season at around 2% CLV, which is about $800 of expected value at a $200 stake, scaling with stake until limits bite.

### Kambi is one feed in the NFL and two prices in college

I tested the vendor-cluster hypothesis on the system's own hourly snapshots: 191 files, about one week, Sept 29 to Oct 6, 2026. **In the NFL, BetRivers and Bally Bet posted identical moneylines in 99.8% of pregame snapshots, identical spreads in 97.2% and identical totals in 98.1%.** betPARX matched as well. In college, the same pair posted the **same number but a different price in 93-98% of spread and total snapshots**. BetRivers runs a wider college margin: average two-way hold was 5.46%, against 4.86% at Bally Bet and betPARX. In the NFL all three sat at 4.94%. For comparison, a vendor dataset reported BetRivers' overround above 8% during college games ([iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/market-making-opportunities-2026/)). This is one week of data, but the pattern supports three rules. Count the Kambi skins as **one opportunity in NFL CLV accounting**, so three copies are not scored as three edges. Treat them as **separate accounts for capacity and limits**. And in college, **always shop Bally Bet against BetRivers**, because the same number usually comes about 0.6 points of hold cheaper. As a control, BetOnline and LowVig shared moneylines exactly but not spread prices, which confirms the repo's finding that its old "sharp median" was effectively one book group.

### The college opener window pays most, and the system polls it least

The same backtest bets, grouped by the Arizona weekday of the snapshot, show **Sunday snapshots earning +5.6% mean CLV (522 bets)**. Every other day earned +0.7% to +2.6%. Sunday was the best day in five of six seasons (2021-26), with 2023 the exception. By horizon, bets 5-7 days out earned +3.7%, against +0.9-1.1% at 1-3 days. This fits Circa posting the first college numbers every Sunday at low limits ([Circa](https://www.circalasvegas.com/blog/sportsbook/from-the-experts/article/how-lines-are-set-an-oddsmakers-perspective/)), and Spanky's report that the best college prices are gone once lines open widely, so his window is Monday morning ET ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/whats-the-difference-between-you-and-a-sharp-step-inside-the-mind-of-a-professional-bettor)). The live cfb_shop track polls **hourly Thursday to Saturday but only every 3 hours otherwise** (`cfb_shop_rules.json`). Because the finding is post-hoc, the clean step is a pre-registered polling change: hourly college snapshots from Sunday 12:00 to Tuesday 12:00 Arizona time. Grade it by comparing CLV and bet count with the 3-hour schedule over the rest of 2026. The cost is Odds API credits; the NFL hourly watch runs about 2,900 credits a month (README).

### Stale prices matter only right after a sharp move

Static NFL shopping fails because NFL soft books copy Pinnacle. The untested variant asks whether a soft book is *stale* rather than whether it holds a different opinion. Pre-register it in two steps. First, measure catch-up on the hourly archive. For each Pinnacle move of at least 1.0 point (spread), 1.5 points (total) or 3 percentage points (no-vig ML) between snapshots, record the share of each book or cluster still unmatched one and two snapshots later, split by NFL vs college and by hour of day. That covers the overnight and Saturday-morning hypotheses in the notes. Second, freeze a rule if any cluster shows a lag. The rule: bet any allowed book whose own line has not moved since the earlier snapshot, at EV ≥ 2% against the new Pinnacle Shin fair. The NFL near miss (+2.5% CLV on 77 all-AZ spread bets at EV ≥ 4%, 4/4 seasons) suggests some NFL opportunities are of this kind. The same design covers two cheap logging studies. In **news freezes**, a book keeps its old price after `news_llm.jsonl` first logs a QB signal. In **tout steam**, a soft book moves toward a published pick in `picks_log.jsonl` while Pinnacle does not, leaving the other side cheap. All three are logging-only, so they are compatible with the frozen news rules.

There are two honest limits. No public study measures US copy latency in football. A vendor's claim of 15-60 seconds for DraftKings and FanDuel is marketing ([SharpAPI](https://sharpapi.io/learn/how-live-is-real-time-odds-data)). Hourly snapshots will see only lags of an hour or more. Execution would need event-driven polling, such as 5-minute pulls for 30 minutes after a news signal or a Pinnacle move. And betting stale numbers right after sharp moves is the classic steam fingerprint, so a live version should be capped to a small share of each account's bets.

### Promotions are the only edge that is positive by construction

A boost is the book knowingly giving up price. Measured against a devigged fair line, documented examples include a FanDuel boost at +380 against fair +314 (15.9% EV) and a Caesars boost at +125 against +104 (9% EV, $25 maximum). Maximum stakes are typically $25-100, and DraftKings reportedly cuts some accounts' boost maximums to $5 ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/education/bonus-hunting-for-existing-sportsbook-users)). Not every boost is +EV. A BetMGM same-game-parlay boost to +250 was still about 11% −EV against an estimated fair of +320, and two rival books' unboosted prices were better ([TheLines](https://www.thelines.com/sportsbook-promo-odds-boost-fool-bettors-nfl-mlb-nba-nhl-2023/)). The engine is simple. EV per $1 = p_fair × (1 + b(1 + boost)) − 1, where p_fair comes from the Pinnacle Shin price the system already computes. A profit-boost token belongs on the lowest-hold main market at that book. My arithmetic: 8 books × one +EV boost per football weekend × $25-50 × about 10% EV comes to roughly **$500-1,000 per season**. That is small, but it carries almost no model risk. Its larger value is camouflage, since boost-taking accounts look recreational while they carry CLV-positive shop bets. Boosts are not in the Odds API, so the practical build is a "price this boost" form on the dashboard plus a ledger. Scraping book menus likely breaks terms of service.

### Thin prop and ladder markets remain hypotheses

Two more book-structure ideas are worth one study each. **Fading Caesars' prop over-moves** rests on Establish The Run's practitioner claim, and Caesars still takes about $500 on props from sharp accounts. The rule: when Caesars moves a prop by at least 1 unit more than the median of three or more other books, take the other side at Caesars if it beats the others' no-vig median by at least 2%. Testing it needs a Caesars account and two or three extra intraday prop pulls per event, at about 3 credits each. **Alt-ladder shape** is the other. Buys are never +EV (`buy_costs.json`), and opening ladders are no softer than later ones. But nobody has tested *selling* through dead numbers (NFL 5, 8, 9, 11), or whether college ladders reuse NFL-shaped increments that `cfb_dist.json` would price differently. The 2023-25 alt data already exists, so this costs about a day. The only external evidence is generic explainers ([LSR](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/sports-betting/alternate-lines/)), so the prior is low.

## Exchanges pay whoever sells the longshot and supplies depth

### Arizona access could end on a court order

Kalshi's Arizona position is fragile. Arizona filed 20 criminal counts in March 2026. A federal judge enjoined enforcement in May. Then on Aug 28, 2026 the Ninth Circuit held in *KalshiEX v. Assad* that "Congress did not take a wrecking ball to all sports gambling regulations" ([Ninth Circuit opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/28/25-7516.pdf)). Arizona has asked the court to dissolve its injunction. The mandate is paused while Kalshi seeks en banc rehearing ([Arizona Mirror](https://azmirror.com/2026/09/25/after-9th-circuit-ruling-mayes-pushes-to-revive-her-gambling-case-against-kalshi/)). Other venues: ProphetX is listed as available in Arizona ([OddsAssist](https://oddsassist.com/prediction-markets/prophetx-states/)), Novig restricts Arizona ([LSR](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/prediction-markets/novig-promo-code/)), and Polymarket US names Arizona only as a state that "may get access later" ([SailGP guide](https://sailgp.com/prediction-markets/polymarket/legal)). Every exchange idea below should hold only capital the user can afford to see frozen for 30 days or more. A docket watch that pauses the Kalshi tracks automatically when the mandate issues is cheap insurance.

### The fee math favors resting bids on favorites

Kalshi's July 2026 schedule charges NFL and NCAAF game markets a taker fee of 0.07 × P(1−P) and a maker fee of 0.0175 × P(1−P) per contract, rounded up ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf?)). At 50¢ that is 1.75¢ for a taker (3.5% of stake) and about 0.44¢ for a maker (0.9%). At 80¢ it is 1.12¢ (1.4%) and 0.28¢ (0.35%). The best study of Kalshi returns covers 2021 to April 2025 and is not football-specific. Makers averaged −9.64% and takers −31.46%. Contracts at 10¢ or below lost over 60%, and **makers buying at 50¢ or above earned about +2.6%**, at a time when makers paid no fee ([Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). Netting today's maker fee leaves an estimated +1.7% to +2.3% on the favorite side, if the bias is unchanged. It is weakening: the price coefficient fell from 0.034 to 0.021 in 2025. The existing `kalshi_maker` track bids both sides at Pinnacle Shin fair − 3¢. The cheap new test is a **second frozen version that rests bids only on the ≥0.70 side, never buys below 0.15, and nets the rounded fee on the posted batch size**. Grade it on post-fee ROI and on CLV against the Pinnacle close, with a pass bar of ROI > 0 at p < 0.05 over at least 300 fills. Paper fills cannot capture queue position, so a simulated pass justifies only a small live allocation, not scale.

### Liquidity rewards may beat trading edge for a small maker

Kalshi's Liquidity Incentive Program runs to Jan 1, 2027. It pays **$1-1,000 per market per day**, split pro rata by a once-per-second sample of two-sided resting depth at or near a reference price. A companion program pays makers in prop legs 25% of combo fees ([Kalshi LIP](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program); [Kalshi combo-leg LIP](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/17184676-sports-prop-combo-market-component-legs-liquidity-incentive-program)). About 95% of Kalshi matches come from 2,000+ small makers, and Kalshi's in-house market maker is reportedly unprofitable ([Turbine](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/how-to-market-make-prediction-markets-2026)). The illustrative arithmetic: a $500-a-day NFL market where the user holds 5% of qualifying depth 80% of the time pays about $20 a day. The first step is measurement only: scrape the active reward pools on KXNFLGAME and KXNCAAFGAME pages for two weeks. Build only if pools are large. A real build needs an order-book WebSocket, a sharp price feed faster than hourly, and a quote-pulling rule tied to `news_llm`, and it should be graded on three separate P&Ls: spread, adverse selection (markout 5 and 60 minutes after each fill) and rewards. This is the highest-turnover idea in the report, so it carries the most tax drag.

### Retail combo buyers fund the other side

Kalshi combos are where retail losses concentrate. Across 12,639 cross-game parlays, combos of two to four legs priced near the independence product, but overpricing grew about **2.9% per leg beyond that**, and parlays won 2-10 percentage points less often than their price implied ([arXiv 2607.14430](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14430v1)). After Kalshi cut the minimum combo price to $0.0001 on Sept 3, 2026, **buyers of sub-1¢ combos lost $4.6M, 86% of stake** ([RG.org](https://rg.org/news/gambling-industry/kalshi-record-volume-longshot-parlays-house-edge)). The edge sits with whoever sells 5+ leg combos. Two unknowns block it: whether retail accounts can answer RFQs, and the tail risk of selling many-leg parlays. Combo makers also pay double the maker fee. It belongs on a watchlist with one action: confirm RFQ access. The same paper found Kalshi prices well calibrated 30-240 minutes before expiry but compressed in the final ten minutes (Platt slopes 1.6-4.6 in NBA, MLB and NHL), meaning late favorites are underpriced. That is testable for the NFL on archived Kalshi trades against nflfastR win probability. But the repo's `live_pilot` showed how a stale-quote timing artifact can manufacture +21% ROI, so any test must align game state to the trade's own timestamp minus a buffer from the first run.

Two cheap logging counters round this out. One checks **ladder monotonicity**: Kalshi's "win by more than X" price must fall as X rises, and any crossed quotes after fees are riskless if the settlement terms match. The other checks **cross-venue middles** between the Kalshi ladder and Arizona alt spreads, priced with `margins.py`. The Odds API already carries Kalshi, Polymarket and ProphetX quotes; my one-week check found them in most NFL snapshots. Expect both counters to come back empty. Kalshi's NFL straight-bet vig (4.32%) sits beside FanDuel's (4.44%) and DraftKings' (4.51%) ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/kalshi-edges-fanduel-draftkings-sportsbooks-in-nfl-week-1-pricing-sept-16-2026)), and the system's own sportsbook arbitrage study found about 0.1 unit a season. DFS pick'em has a well-documented method: play only legs whose sharp no-vig probability beats the 54-58% per-leg break-even ([BettingPros](https://www.bettingpros.com/articles/how-to-make-money-on-prizepicks-picking-the-right-slip-size-props/)). But Arizona moved to revoke Underdog's license in December 2025 ([LSR](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/?p=249145)), so it stays off the list until legality is confirmed.

## New information and model angles worth one honest test

### College availability reports are new, official and unexploited

The SEC, Big 12, ACC, Mountain West and CFP now require availability reports, and the Big Ten's 2026 rule adds reports on Wednesday, Thursday and Friday by 8 p.m. ET plus a gameday report two hours before kickoff. Categories include "out for first half," and visiting teams must also list two-deep players not traveling ([Santa Fe New Mexican/AP](https://www.santafenewmexican.com/sports/big-ten-will-release-4-player-availability-reports-per-week-for-games-matching-conference-teams/article_b2453480-1e2a-5983-a8c1-9e651f7726cb.html); [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/big-12-to-mandate-player-availability-reports-for-football-basketball-starting-in-2025-26)). The reports are timestamped and free, and they cover conference games only. That leaves non-conference games as a built-in control group for any before-and-after efficiency comparison. The build copies `news_audit`. Scrape the conference pages hourly from Wednesday to Saturday. Log the first-seen time per player. Weight each listing by starter usage from CFBD. Then measure Pinnacle's move from first-seen to close for starters listed doubtful or out. The counter-evidence is the repo's own: across 965 college QB changes, Pinnacle moved only 0.24 points after Sunday or Monday (`cfb/round2.md`), so QB news is mostly priced before Wednesday. Whatever edge exists is in non-QB starters, gameday "game-time decision" resolutions and lower-profile games. Run it as logging only for the rest of 2026, and freeze a betting rule only if moves after first-seen average at least 0.5 points over 100 or more events.

### Totals lag rule changes, but by less than the notes claimed

The research notes argued the market under-adjusted to the dynamic kickoff. Their arithmetic (22 drives × +0.19 points per drive ≈ +4 points) ignores that drives per game fell. The source data supports a smaller effect. Points per drive rose from 1.88 (2023) to 2.07 (2024), and the 2024 season produced **622 more points than 2023, about +2.3 per game over 272 games**, while the average posted total rose 1.5 (43.1 to 44.6). In 2025 both scoring (+0.2 per game) and totals (+0.2) barely moved ([VSiN](https://vsin.com/nfl/how-have-the-new-nfl-kickoff-rules-impacted-scoring-and-field-position/)). That puts the season-average lag at most about 0.8 points in 2024, assuming 2023 totals were unbiased, and they were not: unders hit 61.3% through Week 6 of 2023 ([theScore](https://www.thescore.com/nfl/news/2740613)). History supports the pattern as recurring. After college football's 2006 clock change, overs fell to 47.2% and recovered by November ([Paulson](https://www.westga.edu/~bquest/2008/football08.pdf)). The test takes about an hour with nflverse and CFBD data. Compute actual minus closing total by week block (1-4, 5-9, 10+) for NFL 2023-25 and college 2023-24. If early-block bias appears, pre-register a generic rule now: after any future kickoff, overtime or clock change, bet the first four weeks only when a simulator-derived scoring change exceeds the opener's change by 1.5 points or more, graded on CLV. The 2026 NFL changes, such as declarable onside kicks and a closed out-of-bounds loophole, look too small to matter ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/nfl-rule-changes-2026)). The 2025 two-possession overtime rule ([NBC/AP](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/sports/nfl/nfl-owners-rule-changes-overtime-rules-kickoff-touchback-tush-push-2025/6207820/)) shifts push rates at 3 and 6, which makes it a pricing-hygiene fix for `margins.py` rather than an edge.

### Prop boards contain cross-market signals nobody has checked

Two internal identities in the prop board are untested and use data the system already stores in `history/props_*.json.gz`. The first is a **props-implied team total**. Sum each team's no-vig anytime-TD probabilities into expected touchdowns, add field goals from kicker points or a fitted ratio, and compare the result with the team total implied by the game spread and total. A gap of 1.5 points or more is a candidate bet on the team or game total, graded on CLV. A lead-lag test on the 2026 hourly snapshots shows which market moves first. The anytime-TD feed carries only the Yes price, so the devig rests on a calibrated overround assumption. The second is the **passing identity**. A QB's passing yards equal the sum of his receivers' receiving yards, so his median line should roughly match the posted receivers' medians scaled by their share of targets. When the two disagree, the market that moved toward consistency by the close was the stale one. The likeliest payoff from both is a timing filter rather than a standalone edge, as with the totals study, where model disagreement predicted line moves but did not beat the close.

**Kicker props in forecast wind** extend the system's wind infrastructure to a vendor-priced, low-attention market: unders on field goals made and longest field goal when the bet-time forecast is at least 15 mph at an open stadium. The college wind result is a real warning, since books already charged for windy unders on game totals. The hypothesis survives only if kicker props are set from season averages by a vendor that ignores weather. The first step is to confirm the Odds API kicker market keys and 2023-25 history.

### Old anomalies and attention data rate a quick re-test at most

Several documented anomalies are cheap to re-test with CFBD and nflverse, but each has a low prior. Service-academy unders went **44-9-1 since 2006**, and the market has already cut those totals from about 51 to 35 ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/ncaaf/service-academy-betting-trends-over-under-total)). Backing power-conference teams against mid-majors returned +2.94% in 2002-11 ([Krieger et al.](https://ircommons.uwf.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-power-of-wagering-on-power/99380090342306600)). Teams that beat their win total by 3+ went under the next year **64.3%** of the time in 2000-18 ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/nfl-season-win-totals-public-betting-biases-bears-saints-2019)). The last one fits the passing `preseason_prior` track as a single pre-declared feature. Public-money and attention data have the weakest case. DraftKings handle above 65% on one side covered 48.3% over two-plus seasons, but VSiN's subgroup systems are mined without pre-registration ([VSiN](https://vsin.com/nfl/nfl-week-18-betting-splits-systems-update/)). Simple contrarian rules on 3,625 games could not reject zero profit ([Paul & Weinbach](https://kylewoodward.com/blog-data/pdfs/references/paul+weinbach-international-journal-of-sport-finance-2008A.pdf)). Google Trends predicted margins beyond the close in-sample but beat no 52.38% hurdle out of sample ([Gidumal & Muench](https://sites.duke.edu/djepapers/files/2017/06/shivgidumalrolandmuench-dje.pdf)). Logging DraftKings splits next to the odds snapshots costs almost nothing. Any rule built on them should be expected to fail.

Some markets should stay off the list. **College player props are prohibited at Arizona books**: the regulator lists "collegiate proposition bets" among banned wagers ([AZ Dept of Gaming](https://gaming.az.gov/node/1255)). **First-touchdown scorer** markets carried 22-65% hold by book in 2022 ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/best-sportsbooks-first-touchdown-anytime-td-props)), and a results-graded test needs thousands of bets. **Same-game parlays** carry 15-25% hold, and their prices are not in any feed the system has. The one peer-reviewed correlation, that college favorites who cover tend to go over ([Davis, Dawson & Krieger](https://ircommons.uwf.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Correlated-parlay-betting-An-analysis-of/99380090298406600)), reports no returns. At most it justifies an 8-week manual quote log against a CFBD joint-probability table. Beards and shared accounts break terms of service and Arizona rules, and they are how Walters and Spanky historically scaled.

## Conclusion

The new finding is that this system's largest untapped edge is not hidden at all. It is the gap between the books the college track prices and the books the user can bet, and that gap is measured in the system's own files. The vendor map adds a twist the notes only guessed at. Kambi skins really are one feed in the NFL, but in college they quote the same number at different prices, so a cluster can be stale and still offer a choice of price. The exchange and promo ideas share a deeper logic. They spend the system's one real asset, a trustworthy Pinnacle-anchored fair price, in places where winning is either not punished or not visible.

The honest limits are just as clear. Outside the system's own studies, almost nothing here has football-specific measured evidence. The best external numbers come from Kalshi in other sports, practitioner interviews and boost anecdotes. The binding constraints are now account life, Arizona's court docket and a tax rule that penalizes turnover more than forecasting skill. The right posture is to measure everything that costs nothing, pre-register what survives, and treat each live account and exchange balance as a budget to spend deliberately rather than a bankroll to grow.
