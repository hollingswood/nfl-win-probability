# Hunt decorrelated information, not better ratings

A public-data team-strength model, however carefully built, is very unlikely to beat the NFL closing line. The project's own numbers fit what the academic record, the best public models and the pros themselves all report. Among the things the project has not yet tried, the modeling changes with real evidence behind them all do one of three things. They move the bet earlier than the close and grade it by closing-line value (CLV). They make the model's errors *decorrelated* from the market's, instead of making it more accurate. Or they shift effort to college football, where the only peer-reviewed model-beats-line result of the last decade comes from. The theory makes the verdict precise: under Hubáček & Šír's Kelly identity, expected log-growth equals the market's log loss minus the model's. On the project's 2020-25 numbers that is about **−0.014 nats per game for the standalone model and −0.002 for the blend**, so no amount of bet selection can rescue betting either one at the close. The top priorities below are: an ensemble of independent public CFB ratings bet at openers in weeks 2-9; a "news-state" gate that throws out model-vs-market disagreements the market can probably explain; a model whose target is predicted CLV in cover-probability units, with explicit key-number payoffs; and a decorrelation diagnostic and training objective. Two process facts frame all of this. The project's 2022-25 CFB and 2020-25 NFL holdouts are now spent, so new tests need a forward-live holdout starting with the rest of the 2026 season. And edges that claim the *close itself* is wrong can only be confirmed on outcomes, which takes thousands of bets that a decade of NFL games does not supply.

## The close already knows what a public model knows

Forty years of published NFL models have **matched or trailed the market, never beaten it out of sample**. Harville's 1980 procedure picked 70% of winners to the market's 72%. Glickman and Stern's Bayesian state-space model, the ancestor of the project's Kalman ratings, was only "comparable to the betting line" ([Stekler et al. review](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2009-002.pdf)). The largest head-to-head test is Song, Boulier and Stekler's 31,000 forecasts from 70 experts and 32 statistical systems in 2000-01. Both groups went **exactly 50% against the spread**, and the authors found "no evidence that either statistical systems or experts consistently outperform the market" ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v23y2007i3p405-413.html)). Baker and McHale's point-process scoring model was "marginally outperformed by the betting market" on game outcomes ([Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1432547/forecasting-exact-scores-in-national-football-league-games)). Recent ML papers that claim 80-95% NFL accuracy either make no market comparison or show signs of leakage ([arXiv 2410.21484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21484)). The college version is the CFBD community's 714-feature gradient-boosted model: RMSE rose from 15.72 in development to 16.77 in production, and it went **64-63 ATS (50%) live** ([CFBD blog](https://blog.collegefootballdata.com/predicting-spreads-gbdt/)). That is the same failure the project saw with its own GBM.

Hubáček and Šír explain why. A bettor who Kelly-stakes model probabilities T against market probabilities M grows wealth at W = D_KL(R‖M) − D_KL(R‖T). In expectation that is the market's log loss minus the model's. A model can profit while being *less* accurate than the market only if its errors are decorrelated from the market's errors given the truth, so that the bets it chooses are the ones where it beats the market ([arXiv 2010.12508](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12508)). Applying the identity to the project's out-of-sample numbers (my arithmetic) gives 0.607 − 0.621 = **−0.014 nats per game** for the model and **−0.002** for the blend. The blend result is the telling one. A logistic blend that adds nothing over the close means the model's information is close to a subset of the market's. More features of the same kind (EPA, QB quality, matchups, havoc) feed more of the same information into the model. That is why the long list of tested inputs all failed in the same way.

The pros say the same thing in their own words. Rufus Peabody puts the best combination at **"45 percent MP, 55 percent market,"** bets only when model and market disagree by about two points, and concedes that bookmakers do better at "rating specific players and factoring in qualitative information" ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article.php?id=40172)). nfelo's tuned optimum is **65% market / 35% model** ([nfelo](https://www.nfeloapp.com/analysis/using-market-regression-to-improve-prediction-accuracy-in-the-nfl/)), and its MAE is within **0.01 points of the close** ([nfelo performance](https://nfeloapp.com/games/nfl-model-performance/)). Spanky, a full-time syndicate bettor, says his team spent "seven, eight years" building software to detect injuries and line moves, and that the scoreboard is "trying to beat that closing number" ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/whats-the-difference-between-you-and-a-sharp-step-inside-the-mind-of-a-professional-bettor)). None of these people claims a better team-strength regression is the edge. Levitt's 2004 contest data adds that past bettor performance did not predict future performance ([Levitt 2004](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/users/aldous/157/Papers/Levitt_Gambling_2004.pdf)).

The project's CFB close-residual GBM top quintile went 52.5% on the 2022-25 holdout. That sits inside SP+'s documented 51-53.5% range, so it is near the practical ceiling for public CFB ratings rather than an outright failure. Its 47.4% on opener disagreements of five or more points matches Connelly's and Peabody's experience. Small disagreements are noise, as Connelly's 11-16-1 sub-one-point bucket shows, and large ones are often information the model lacks.

## Residual signal survives in timing, not in better forecasts of the score

Wherever an edge looks real in the evidence, it is either an **open-to-close timing edge** or a **thin-market edge**. Moskowitz's 59,592-game study finds that recent-performance momentum predicts open-to-close moves, and that those moves then fully reverse from close to outcome. The effects are only about a fifth the size of their financial-market counterparts and are "easily wiped out by trading costs" ([NBER](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f92072/f92072.pdf)). Avery and Chevalier found NFL line moves predictable from sentiment available before the opener, with contrarian betting only "borderline" profitable ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jnlbus/v72y1999i4p493-521.html)). In CFB bowls, following 3-plus-point moves went **59.5% against the opener but 54% against the close** ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaaf/bowl-game-betting-tips-has-following-big-line-moves-worked)). Coleman's metamodel was significant only against openers. Practitioners also say CFB lines "stay soft longer," with lower limits, though that comes from an unsourced industry article ([Business of College Sports](https://businessofcollegesports.com/sports-betting/how-college-football-betting-differs-from-the-nfl-and-which-numbers-matter/)).

Published anomalies decay once they are documented. The highest-paid-coach CFB edge fell from 53.95% to **51.6% in its last five years** ([Farinella & Moffett](https://www.westga.edu/~bquest/2016/football2016.pdf)). The CFB totals-overs bias "largely corrected" by 2003-15 ([Francisco & Moore](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jecfin/v42y2018i4d10.1007_s12197-018-9437-y.html)). After the 2006 clock-rule change cut about 14 plays a game, the market took most of one season to adjust: overs ran 47.23% that year but recovered to 49.2% by November ([Paulson 2008](https://www.westga.edu/~bquest/2008/football08.pdf)). Gray and Gray's NFL probit edges confirmed only partially out of sample, and "inefficiencies…tend to dissipate over time" ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v52y1997i4p1725-37.html)). The project's three frozen CFB rules failing on the 2022-25 holdout is what this literature predicts for rules estimated on older data.

CLV itself needs careful handling. In Karl Whelan's 3,670-game NBA study, only the **top CLV decile earned meaningful profit (+11.4%)**. A decile with about 5% CLV returned +0.2%, and three of five positive-CLV deciles lost money, because CLV has to exceed the book's margin ([Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)). The benchmark is also imperfect. Soft-book bets that beat Pinnacle's price were nearly perfectly calibrated over 31,247 soccer bets, but against Pinnacle closes the last two seasons returned **1.9% actual vs 4.3% expected** on 6,806 bets ([Networked](https://networked.substack.com/p/a-view-from-the-pinnacle)). Both studies come from other sports, so the project should check the CLV-to-ROI conversion on its own NFL and CFB data rather than assume it.

## Ten untried modeling changes, ranked by evidence and testability

The ranking weighs four things: how strong the evidence is, whether the change could plausibly carry information the close lacks, whether it can be tested under the project's discipline in a realistic time, and cost. Two definitions recur. A **Type A** edge beats the opener because the close is right and arrives later; CLV tests it efficiently. A **Type B** edge claims the close itself is wrong; only outcomes can test it, and at NFL volumes that takes many years.

| Rank | Change | Edge type | Evidence strength | Data cost | Realistic test horizon |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CFB metamodel of independent public ratings, bet at open, weeks 2-9 | A | Peer-reviewed (vs openers) | Free | 1 forward season (CLV) |
| 2 | News-state gate on all disagreement signals | A | Indirect, but strong and consistent | Free (NFL); hand-curated (CFB) | Re-analysis now + forward season |
| 3 | Predicted-CLV target with key-number payoff, extreme-tail betting | A | Theory + project's own correlations | Free; timestamped odds help (paid) | 1 forward season |
| 4 | Decorrelation diagnostic and training objective | A/B | Peer-reviewed theory; NBA application | Free | Diagnostic immediately |
| 5 | Sparse positional availability model timed to practice reports | A | Practitioner only | Free; PFF Pro $199.99/yr optional | Needs timestamped midweek lines |
| 6 | Preseason-anchoring prior, NFL weeks 2-8 | B | Peer-reviewed 2025, abstract only | Low (archived futures) | Many seasons (outcomes) |
| 7 | Dynamic market-shrinkage weight | A | Vendor analysis | Free | Immediate walk-forward |
| 8 | CFB structural recalibration (HFA trend, time-zone dogs) | B | Peer-reviewed | Free | Many seasons (outcomes) |

| 10 | FTN charting features | A/B | None vs market | Free | Short history (2022+) |

### 1. Ensemble independent public CFB ratings and bet the opener in weeks 2-9

**Evidence.** Coleman (2025, *Journal of Sports Analytics*) combined 29 rating systems from The Prediction Tracker over **5,925 games in 2016-24** into a five-system metamodel. It was statistically significant against **opening** lines in both validation and test samples ([abstract](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dfb/Record/4094978?lng=en); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/22150218251365223)). This is the most recent peer-reviewed model-beats-line result in either sport, and it comes from ensembling rather than one better rating. SP+'s per-season record shows a single strong public rating alone hovers near break-even ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/college-football/insider/story/_/id/32250032/numbers-game-how-sp+-other-ratings-give-betting-edge)).

**Why it might carry signal.** Independent systems (Sagarin, Massey-style margin ratings, EPA-based, recruiting-heavy) make partly uncorrelated errors. Averaging them cuts the idiosyncratic noise any one system, including the project's own opponent-adjusted ratings, brings to the opener. Per Hubáček & Šír, that is the decorrelation the project's single models lack. The edge is Type A: the CFB close is probably already about as good as the ensemble, which is why Coleman's significance holds only at the open.

**Data.** The Prediction Tracker publishes about 30 systems next to opening, midweek and updated lines ([Prediction Tracker](https://www.thepredictiontracker.com/predncaa.html)). It is free, but this research could not confirm that its archive is deep enough for a backtest. If it is not, the project must start collecting each system's weekly predictions now.

**Test.** Freeze the ensemble (system selection, weights by inverse trailing error, a minimum disagreement threshold) using only pre-2022 data. Do not reuse the spent 2022-25 holdout for the decision. Use it only as a sanity check, and run the decisive test forward-live on the rest of 2026 and on 2027. At roughly 650 FBS games a season (5,925 games over nine seasons), weeks 2-9 give about 400 games. Betting the top 15-20% of disagreements yields about **60-80 bets per season**. That is enough to detect a mean CLV of about +0.3 points (below) but nowhere near enough to confirm an ATS win rate. The pass rule is CLV in the top predicted decile that exceeds the vig hurdle, not the mean.

### 2. Gate every disagreement signal on "news state" to remove adverse selection

**Evidence.** The project's own 47.4% on 5-plus-point CFB opener disagreements is the clearest data point: big gaps are where the model is most often missing information. Peabody notes that "the ratings know if a starting QB is out — but they don't know if he is playing through an injury" ([Betting Life](https://betting-life-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/rufus-dawgs-name)). CFB bowl moves are driven by opt-out and portal news that reaches insiders before it is public ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/ncaaf/bowl-game-betting-tips-has-following-big-line-moves-worked)). Walters ranks injuries as the second-most-important factor ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/guides/betting-tips-from-pro-sports-bettor-billy-walters)).

**Why it might carry signal.** This does not add information. It removes the bets where the market *has* information the model lacks. Disagreements in games with clean news states (no questionable starters, no QB uncertainty, no unexplained early move) are more likely to be model edges than missing data. Of the ten changes, this is the one most likely to turn the existing opener correlation of 0.1-0.3 into usable selection.

**Data.** For the NFL, nflverse `load_injuries` (2009+, with practice participation) and `load_depth_charts` (2001+) are free ([nflreadr manual](https://cran.nics.utk.edu/cran/web/packages/nflreadr/nflreadr.pdf)). For CFB there is no public injury feed, so QB status, opt-out and portal flags must be curated by hand. That is costly in time but plausible for a weeks 2-9 subset.

**Test.** Pre-register the gate definition first: a starting QB listed as questionable or limited, two or more starters questionable at the same position group, or an open-to-current move of 1.5 or more points against the model before bet time. Then re-score the existing opener signals as two frozen groups, gated-in and gated-out. The hypothesis is directional: gated-in CLV exceeds gated-out CLV. Because the signals already exist, this can be run on historical data immediately, but it must be labeled exploratory there, with the confirmatory test forward-live. Use a one-sided test on the CLV difference. With per-bet CLV SD of about one point, about 150 bets per arm detects a 0.3-point difference.

### 3. Re-target the opener model to predicted CLV in cover-probability units, and bet only the extreme tail

**Evidence.** The project found that model-vs-open gaps correlate 0.1-0.3 with line moves but don't cover. The arithmetic shows why (my calculation from sourced inputs). Near pick'em, one point is worth about 3 percentage points of cover probability (normal SD of margin minus line ≈ 13.59 ([arXiv 1211.4000](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.4000))). Clearing the 52.38% breakeven therefore needs an expected favorable move of about **0.8 points off key numbers, or about 0.5 points through 3 or 7**. In 2002-11 more than 2,000 of 2,560 NFL games moved one point or less. With a move SD near one point, a correlation of 0.3 and a signal two standard deviations out predict only about 0.6 points of movement, and a correlation of 0.1 predicts 0.2. The average signal cannot pay. Only the extreme tail, or moves that cross a key number, can. Margin distributions are lumpy: 3 is about 14.8% of games, and 5, 6 and 8 have grown since the 2015 PAT change ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/nfl-key-betting-numbers-spread-margins-of-victory-line-value); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/nfl/key-numbers)). Whelan's decile result says profit concentrates in the top CLV decile ([Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)).

**Why it might carry signal.** The current approach treats a disagreement in points as the signal. The payoff is actually the *cover-probability value* of the expected move, which depends heavily on whether the move crosses 3 or 7. A model trained directly on that target (close-implied cover probability minus open-implied cover probability, computed from an empirical P(margin = k | spread)) puts its capacity where the money is. Moskowitz's momentum result supplies a feature family that is documented to predict moves: recent performance and streaks known before the opener ([NBER](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f92072/f92072.pdf)).

**Data.** Opener and close for both sports are already in hand. A smoothed empirical margin distribution by spread bucket, era-adjusted for after 2015, follows nfelo's published method ([nfelo](https://www.nfeloapp.com/analysis/margin-probabilities-from-nfl-spreads/)). Timestamped intermediate prices would let the model learn *when* to bet. Whelan used The-Odds-API, a paid feed whose cost this research did not establish.

**Test.** The primary metric is realized CLV in cover-probability units on the top decile of predicted CLV. The decision rule is that the top-decile mean exceeds 2.38 percentage points with a one-sided 95% bound. The NFL gives about 27 top-decile bets per season, so the sample has to pool NFL sides, NFL totals and CFB, or run two forward seasons. Measure sample size in bets, not games.

### 4. Measure and then train for decorrelation from the market

**Evidence.** Hubáček & Šír show that profit requires a low partial correlation Corr[T, M | R], and that the market taker's ability to choose sides is a structural advantage ([arXiv 2010.12508](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12508)). Hubáček, Šourek & Železný (IJF 2019) made decorrelation from bookmaker odds an explicit training objective in the NBA ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v35y2019i2p783-796.html)). Calibration-optimized model selection outperformed accuracy-optimized selection on returns in NBA work cited by a 2024 review ([arXiv 2410.21484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21484)). There is no NFL or CFB application.

**Why it might carry signal.** Every model the project has tested was optimized for accuracy, which pushes it toward copying the market. A decorrelation penalty, or training on margin minus open using only features orthogonalized to the open, pushes the model toward whatever independent information it has. It is the theoretically correct objective, but the expected payoff is small: the 0.609 blend says the independent information is close to zero.

**Data.** Free. Existing model outputs and lines are enough.

**Test.** First, compute the partial correlation of each existing model's errors with the market's errors, by season, for every model already built. This costs nothing and tells the project which of its models, if any, holds independent information worth weighting. Then train with loss = log loss + λ × Corr(model error, market error), choosing λ only on pre-2020 data, and evaluate forward-live on CLV. Kill it if no existing model shows a partial correlation materially below the others.

### 5. Replace dense player ratings with sparse positional availability values, timed to the practice-report cycle

**Evidence.** Walters' published scheme gives QBs about seven points, top non-QBs 2.5-3, and "basically zero" to about 60% of players. It weights clustered injuries in a fixed priority order: pass catchers, then defensive line, offensive line, defensive backs, linebackers and running backs ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/guides/betting-tips-from-pro-sports-bettor-billy-walters)). R.J. White uses 10-plus points for elite-QB-to-backup changes and 0.5-1 for a star WR ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/nfl-betting-on-opening-lines-how-to-build-a-power-rating-system-and-why-its-crucial-to-beating-the-odds)). All of this is practitioner evidence. No peer-reviewed study found the NFL close misprices non-QB injuries.

**Why it might carry signal.** RAPM spreads value across 22 players and cannot recover sparse, nonlinear cluster effects from public data. The real opportunity is Type A: final inactives are in the close, so a practice-report model (Wednesday to Friday participation trajectories) can only beat lines posted *before* the news settles. Against the close it will look priced in by construction, which may explain part of the project's null injury results.

**Data.** nflverse injuries, depth charts, snap counts (2012+) and OverTheCap contracts are free ([nflreadr manual](https://cran.nics.utk.edu/cran/web/packages/nflreadr/nflreadr.pdf)). PFF Pro at **$199.99/yr**, listed as "coming soon" in September 2026, is the cheapest programmatic source of player grades for replacement-level drop-offs, but its historical depth is undocumented ([PFF pricing](https://www.pff.com/news/a-new-chapter-of-pff-pricing); [PFF API](https://developer.pff.com/)). Timestamped midweek lines are essential and paid.

**Test.** Without timestamped Wednesday-Friday lines this cannot be tested honestly, so do not start it until that feed exists. Once it does, compare predicted against realized move from the report timestamp to the close, only on games with a qualifying cluster event. The project should count those events from its own data. Expect a small subset per season, so plan on pooling two or more seasons.

### 6. Test a frozen preseason-anchoring prior in NFL weeks 2-8, and accept that it is outcome-only

**Evidence.** Fodor, Patterson & Shank (2025, *Economics Letters*, 5,088 games 2003-23) find that sportsbooks "continue to incorporate pre-season odds into their closing lines throughout the entire season," and that betting **in line with** preseason Super Bowl odds was significantly profitable in weeks 2-8 ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165176525001259)). That direction is in tension with the holdover-bias and Week-2-overreaction papers ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/buc/jpredm/v9y2015i1p53-67.html)). One reading reconciles them: futures prices fold in roster changes that last season's results do not, and bettors overreact to early in-season results. The direction must still be pre-registered from the full text, not assumed.

**Why it might carry signal.** It is one of the few documented claims that the *close* is biased (Type B), and it uses a cheap, frozen input that differs from the in-season market-implied ratings the project has already built.

**Data.** Archived preseason Super Bowl and win-total prices, which are low cost.

**Test, and the honest limit.** The paper's sample ends in 2023, so 2024-25 is a clean out-of-sample window. But weeks 2-8 contain only about 112 games a season, so 224 games give a 95% confidence interval of about **±6.5 percentage points** on win rate. CLV cannot test it either, because the claim is that the close is wrong. The feasible test is a paired, per-game log-loss comparison between the close and the close adjusted by a frozen anchoring term, accumulated over 2024 onward. Do not stake on it until several seasons agree.

### 7. Replace the fixed 2015-19 blend weight with a dynamic, walk-forward market-shrinkage weight

**Evidence.** nfelo reports that a weight scaled by trailing exponentially weighted model-vs-market error beats a flat weight on Brier score ([nfelo](https://www.nfeloapp.com/analysis/using-market-regression-to-improve-prediction-accuracy-in-the-nfl/)). The evidence is vendor-grade, and the window length and years are undisclosed.

**Why it matters.** The fixed blend's 0.609 out-of-sample result has the signature of regime shift plus a static weight fit in one era. A dynamic weight will not beat the close, but it is the right *prior* for an opener-time model (items 1-3), where the opener, not the close, is the anchor. Using the close as a feature for an early-week bet would be leakage.

**Test.** Refit only on data before each week. Success means opener-time blend log loss below the opener's own log loss in walk-forward. This is cheap and can be checked immediately on history as an engineering choice rather than a betting rule.

### 8. Recalibrate CFB structure: falling home field and late-season time-zone underdogs

**Evidence.** FBS home field in 2023 was **2.39 points** (95% credible interval 1.89-2.88) and is falling about 0.1 points a year. Unadjusted estimates are inflated by paid-for blowouts ([arXiv 2401.16392](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.16392)). Coleman (2017, *Journal of Sports Economics*) found the CFB market "an inaccurate and inefficient processor of travel effects," most clearly for underdogs facing a one-hour time-zone disadvantage late in the season ([UNF](https://scholars.unf.edu/en/publications/team-travel-effects-and-the-college-football-betting-market/)). Bennett (2019) found prior-season AP top-10 teams overvalued in game one, especially against non-Power 5 opponents ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/atlecj/v47y2019i1d10.1007_s11293-019-09611-y.html)).

**Why it matters.** A 3-plus-point CFB home field biases every prediction by 0.5-1 point. That alone could explain part of the 12.92 vs 12.49 MAE gap and feed false disagreements. Check it first; it costs nothing. The travel and holdover effects are Type B and small.

**Test.** HFA is a calibration fix, so verify it with a paired MAE test against the current specification on pre-2022 data. The time-zone rule covers perhaps a few dozen late-season games a year, and Bennett's covers about ten. Neither can be confirmed on outcomes within this decade, so treat them as model terms, not standalone rules.

### 9. Price derivatives off the team model rather than projecting them independently

**Evidence.** Miller and Davidow point bettors to "related, derivative and prop markets with less picked-through prices" ([LSR](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/33079/the-logic-of-sports-betting-market-agreement-and-resistance/)). Prop specialists argue books cannot monitor hundreds of props and react to news more slowly, while limiting winners to "$200-300 (at most)" ([Establish The Run](https://establishtherun.com/miller-why-prop-betting-is-profitable/)). No rigorous study exists.

**Why it matters.** The project's receptions line-shopping edge survived, which fits this view. Team totals, halves and correlated props priced from one joint simulation of margin, total and pace may catch books that price derivatives mechanically off the main line. Limits cap the upside.

**Test.** Grade on CLV against the sharpest available derivative close. Volume is high (dozens of markets a week), so 200-plus bets in one season is realistic.

### 10. Add FTN charting context only after items 1-4

FTN charting (play-action, RPO, screens, blitz) is free in nflverse from 2022 and charted within 48 hours ([nflreadr manual](https://cran.nics.utk.edu/cran/web/packages/nflreadr/nflreadr.pdf)). It is probably under-used, but it has only four seasons of history and no evidence of value against the market. In the decorrelation framing it is more of the same kind of information the market already has. FTN participation data from 2023 onward is released only after the postseason, so using it in a backtest leaks information that is unavailable at bet time.

### What not to pursue

The evidence argues against more features in tree models (CFBD's 714 features went 50%), deep learning on play-by-play (no evidence either way against closes), fading public betting splits (no excess returns in 1,278 games ([Paul, Weinbach & Humphreys](https://sites.ualberta.ca/~econwps/2011/wp2011-16.pdf)), and no archived timestamped splits to backtest), PFF trench grades as a spread input (no study shows value beyond EPA), and any system copied from a tout record without CLV.

## Fresh holdouts, CLV deciles and sequential tests decide everything

The project's tests are valid but now **contaminated by reuse**. Every look at 2022-25 CFB or 2020-25 NFL results makes the next rule tested on that window less trustworthy. The forward 2026-27 seasons are the only clean holdout left, and today, early October 2026, is the moment to freeze rules for the rest of this season. Outcome tests cannot resolve realistic edges at football volumes. CLV tests can, which is why the top-ranked items are all Type A.

| Test | Effect to detect | Bets needed (one-sided α 0.05, power 0.8) | Football equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| ATS win rate | 54.5% vs 52.38% | ~3,500 | ~13 seasons of every NFL game |
| ATS win rate | 53.5% vs 52.38% | ~12,000 | Not achievable |
| Mean CLV (SD ≈ 1 pt) | +0.3 points | ~70 | One season of a selective CFB rule |
| Mean CLV (SD ≈ 1 pt) | +0.15 points | ~275 | One season of pooled NFL+CFB |

These are my calculations from the sourced inputs ([arXiv 1211.4000](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.4000); [Networked](https://networked.substack.com/p/a-view-from-the-pinnacle)). The CLV SD of one point is an assumption based on 2002-11 movement, so the project should compute it from its own opener and close data. Four further rules follow from the evidence. Grade on the **distribution** of CLV, requiring the top decile to clear the vig, because positive mean CLV can coexist with losses ([Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)). Run each frozen rule as a sequential probability ratio test on CLV, so a dead idea is killed in weeks rather than seasons. Control false discoveries across the candidates with Benjamini-Hochberg rather than judging each in isolation. And replace MAE comparisons with two direct tests: regress margin minus line on model minus line, and use paired per-game error differences. Nfelo's MAE parity with the close shows that matching MAE is neither the bar nor a sign of edge.

## Conclusion

The literature and the project's own results agree on the main point. For an NFL side or total at the close, a public-data model is a noisier copy of the price, and the Kelly identity says copies lose. The project's surviving edges are all price-based, which is not a failure of modeling effort. It is the market telling the project where information asymmetry actually exists. The modeling work worth doing changes what the model is for. It should forecast where the price will go, filter out the disagreements the market can explain, and seek errors uncorrelated with the market's rather than smaller errors. College openers are the most promising place to point it. Even there, the best realistic outcome is a narrow, limit-constrained edge confirmed by CLV over one or two forward seasons, not a model that out-predicts the close.

The most useful immediate step costs nothing. Compute the partial correlation of each existing model's errors with the market's errors, and re-score the existing opener signals with a pre-registered news-state gate, before building anything new. If no existing model shows independent error structure, and if gating does not separate CLV, the evidence says to put further effort into execution (line shopping, timing, derivatives) rather than into another rating system.
