# Academic and Quantitative Evidence on Beating NFL / College Football Betting Lines with Models

Scope note: sources below were checked through abstracts, working-paper PDFs, or publisher/RePEc pages fetched during this research (Oct 2026). Where only an abstract was available, specific numbers that are not in the abstract are not reported. "Peer-reviewed" = journal article; "working paper/thesis/vendor" are flagged. Breakeven at standard -110 pricing is 52.38%.

## 1. Which studies find exploitable NFL/CFB inefficiencies, and did they persist out of sample / in recent years?

### Takeaway
The literature documents many statistically significant anomalies (home underdogs, favorite overpricing, Week 1 unders, holdover bias, divisional games, rest/bye, weather/humidity, "hot hand" overpricing), but almost all are (a) identified on historical data with in-sample or one-split tests, (b) small (a few points of win rate above 50%), and (c) where later follow-ups exist, they tend to weaken or disappear ("inefficiencies dissipate over time"; CFB totals bias "largely corrected"). The two biggest-sample, finance-grade studies (Moskowitz; Levitt) find biases that are real but too small to beat the vig, or that are exactly the shading bookmakers deliberately choose up to the point of non-exploitability.

### Cited Findings

**Broad, large-sample / high-rigor studies**
- Moskowitz, "Asset Pricing and Sports Betting" (NBER conference paper; Yale/AQR): 117,442 contracts across 59,592 games; NFL 7,035 games 1985-2013 (spreads, moneylines, totals). Momentum (recent team performance) predicts open-to-close line movement, and that movement fully reverses from close to outcome (delayed overreaction). Value effects weaker; size no effect. Effects ~one-fifth the magnitude of financial-market momentum/value, and "returns to momentum and value are easily wiped out by trading costs." Momentum overreaction strongest early in seasons. — [Moskowitz, NBER PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f92072/f92072.pdf)
- Levitt (2004), Economic Journal: online sportsbook handicapping contest, 2001 NFL season, 285 entrants, 19,770 bets, 242 games. ATS win rates in the contest sample: home underdogs 57.7%, home favorites 49.1%, visiting favorites 47.8%, visiting underdogs 50.4%. Over 1980-2001, visiting favorites covered only 46.7% while drawing 68.2% of bets. Bookmakers do not balance books; they shade prices against favorite-loving bettors, raising gross margins ~20-30%. No evidence of persistent individual bettor skill (past performance did not predict future performance). — [Levitt 2004 PDF](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/users/aldous/157/Papers/Levitt_Gambling_2004.pdf); [NBER w9422 abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9422)
- Gray & Gray (1997), Journal of Finance: probit models on NFL spreads (pre-1997 data) generated statistically significant in-sample profits; out-of-sample confirmation was only partial ("some inconsistency"), and "inefficiencies in this market tend to dissipate over time." Also reported slight market overconfidence in favorites. — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v52y1997i4p1725-37.html); [Stekler et al. review](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2009-002.pdf)

**Home underdog / favorite bias (NFL)**
- Szalkowski & Nelson (2012, arXiv, ODU; not peer-reviewed): 2,560 NFL games 2002-2011. Home teams won 57% SU but covered 48.9%; home favorites covered 47.9%; home underdogs covered 53.5% (just above 52.38% breakeven). In-sample descriptive tabulation, no out-of-sample test. — [arXiv 1211.4000](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.4000)
- Humphreys, Paul & Weinbach (2013), International Journal of Sport Finance: using newer data with actual betting-percentage data, they contradict the earlier explanation that bettors underestimate home underdogs; bettors predictably bet the "best teams," and sportsbooks do not appear to follow the balanced-book model. — [FIT Publishing abstract](https://fitpublishing.com/node/1071); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/jsf/intjsf/v8y2013i4p294-311.html)
- Shank (2018), Journal of Economics and Finance, "Is the NFL betting market still inefficient?": books appear to undervalue home teams, especially large home underdogs that failed to cover recently; totals more likely to go over when closing totals are very small or very large (quadratic), and when home team previously went over. Reports *statistical* inefficiency (abstract does not establish net profitability). — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jecfin/v42y2018i4d10.1007_s12197-018-9431-4.html)
- Shank (2019), Studies in Economics and Finance: 2003-2016 NFL; home teams cover less and unders more likely in divisional-rival games; a model combining this with previously identified anomalies produced out-of-sample win rate "of nearly 57 per cent" in spreads and totals (out-of-sample window details not in abstract; same author/data-mining risk). — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/sefpps/sef-11-2018-0354.html)

**Early-season / holdover / overreaction (NFL)**
- DiFilippo, Krieger, Davis & Fodor (2014), Journal of Sports Economics: 2000-2010 NFL; Week 1 scoring significantly lower, but books did not lower Week 1 totals; betting all Week 1 unders yielded a statistically significant 13.6% return per game. In-sample, ~11 seasons x ~16 games ≈ 176 bets. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/jospec/v15y2014i2p201-211.html)
- Davis, Fodor, McElfresh & Krieger (2015), Journal of Prediction Markets: "multiple market inefficiencies" in NFL Week 2 tied to overreaction to Week 1 results. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/buc/jpredm/v9y2015i1p53-67.html)
- Fodor, DiFilippo, Krieger & Davis, "Inefficient pricing from holdover bias in NFL point spread markets" (Applied Financial Economics) — prior-season reputation over-weighted early in the season. — [UWF record](https://ircommons.uwf.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Inefficient-pricing-from-holdover-bias-in/99380090649806600)
- Avery & Chevalier (1999), Journal of Business: NFL spreads move predictably during the week in response to sentiment proxies (expert picks, momentum/"hot hand," prestige teams) known before the opener; betting against predicted movement was only "borderline" profitable. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jnlbus/v72y1999i4p493-521.html)
- Paul, Weinbach & Humphreys (2011 WP, U. Alberta): 1,278 NFL games 2005-2009 with betting-percentage data from four offshore books; favorites/road favorites and streaking teams (~+3% of bets) attract disproportionate action, books don't balance, but betting against streaking/popular teams produced no significant excess returns (win rates near 50%). — [U. Alberta WP 2011-16](https://sites.ualberta.ca/~econwps/2011/wp2011-16.pdf)

**Rest / situational / weather (NFL)**
- Rest-pattern study (2002-2015 NFL; FIT Publishing, ~2020): logistic model found inefficiencies for Thursday Night Football and post-bye games; post-bye games consistently profitable over the sample; TNF biases did not reliably generate positive returns. — [FIT Publishing](https://fitpublishing.com/node/4153)
- Atmospheric conditions study (FIT Publishing; authors include Paul/Berkowitz per index): humidity significantly explains actual-minus-total scoring; simple strategies on humidity, wind, and combinations "reject market efficiency." — [FIT Publishing](https://fitpublishing.com/node/3503)

**Model-based vs. anomaly**
- Sinha, Dyer, Gimpel & Smith (2013, CMU, arXiv): logistic regression with Twitter features + game stats, strictly online test on weeks 4-16 of 2012 only; best ATS 57.2%, best O/U 58.2%. Single partial season (~190 games), many feature variants tried — high multiple-comparison risk; not replicated. — [arXiv 1310.6998](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.6998)

### Inferences
- The recurring "inefficiencies" are concentrated in exactly the places the project's walk-forward test would not see much signal: very small subsets (Week 1 totals, home dogs of a certain size, post-bye) where 150-500 bets produce wide confidence intervals. A 53.5% rate over ~500 bets has a 95% CI of roughly ±4.4 pp, so it cannot be distinguished from breakeven.
- Levitt and Humphreys/Paul/Weinbach together imply favorite/home-dog shading is a deliberate bookmaker pricing choice bounded by adverse selection — consistent with small, non-robust edges rather than a free lunch, and with retail-book shading not appearing in sharp closing lines.
- Moskowitz's result is the most relevant to the project: overreaction exists but is mostly expressed in open-to-close movement; at the close, the remaining predictable component is too small to beat costs.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed replication of the home-underdog, Week 1 unders, or post-bye anomalies on 2016-2025 NFL data. Their recent status is unknown from the academic literature (vendor/blog trend data exists but was not verified).
- Full-text numbers for Shank 2018/2019, the holdover-bias paper, and the rest/weather papers (exact win rates, n, out-of-sample windows) were not accessible beyond abstracts.

## 2. Opening vs closing line accuracy and line movement predictability (do models beat openers but not closers?)

### Takeaway
Academic evidence on NFL opening vs closing accuracy is thin and mixed: one 2002-2011 study found openers and closers statistically indistinguishable in MSE, while Moskowitz shows open-to-close moves are partly predictable from public signals (momentum) and then reverse. The best public quantitative evidence that "models beat openers but not closers" is non-academic (nfelo), which shows a materially better ATS record vs openers than closers and near-zero MAE gap vs the close.

### Cited Findings
- Szalkowski & Nelson (2012): 2002-2011, the opening line had a slightly smaller MSE than the closing line, but the difference was small and statistically insignificant. SD of (margin - line) ≈ 13.59 points. — [arXiv 1211.4000](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.4000)
- Moskowitz: open-to-close moves correlate with recent performance (momentum) and fully reverse from close to outcome — i.e., some line movement is noise/overreaction, not information. — [NBER PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f92072/f92072.pdf)
- Avery & Chevalier (1999): NFL line movement during the week is predictable from pre-opening sentiment variables; a contrarian strategy was only borderline profitable. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jnlbus/v72y1999i4p493-521.html)
- nfelo (Robby Greer; vendor model site, not peer-reviewed, model partly anchored on market lines and tuned on history): 2009-2026, ~1,920 games vs openers / ~1,760 vs closers: ATS 56.81% vs openers (+169.4u) but 54.34% vs closers (+113.5u); MAE 10.1; MAE vs market +0.13 points worse than the opener and +0.01 vs the close (essentially equal); reported average CLV 6.11%; losing 2024 season (-8.1u open, -11.1u close). — [nfelo model performance](https://nfeloapp.com/games/nfl-model-performance/)
- Song, Boulier & Stekler / Stekler review: 1994-2001, the market picked NFL winners at least 63% of the time each year (~65-66% average). — [Stekler et al. 2009 WP](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2009-002.pdf)

### Inferences
- The nfelo numbers (a fairly simple Elo/market-regressed model) show the pattern directly: positive edge vs openers shrinks against closers, and point-error vs the close is equal, not better. The project's finding (model log loss 0.621 vs Vegas 0.607 at close) is consistent with a well-built public-data model being slightly worse than the close.
- Because NFL closing lines and openers differ only modestly in accuracy, the "edge" available to a public-data model is mainly a timing edge (betting before the market incorporates the same public signals), not an informational edge at the close. CLV vs the close is the right yardstick, and the literature supports that the close is the hardest target.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed NFL study since 2012 measuring opener vs closer accuracy on modern (sharp-originated, e.g., Pinnacle/Circa) lines was found.
- No academic validation that NFL CLV predicts long-run profit was found; claims to that effect are from industry sources (not cited here as evidence).

## 3. Bayesian/state-space, player-based, and ML models evaluated against the market

### Takeaway
Every serious statistical model compared against NFL lines (Harville 1980; Glickman & Stern 1998 state-space; Boulier & Stekler 2003; Song et al. 2007's 32 systems; Baker & McHale 2013 point-process; Warner 2010 Gaussian process) matches or trails the market. ML papers claiming large NFL accuracy (80-95%) lack market comparison or show signs of leakage. The theoretical result most useful to the project is Hubáček & Šír: a model can profit while being less accurate than the bookmaker only if its errors are decorrelated from the bookmaker's — and the expected Kelly growth equals the bookmaker's KL-divergence from truth minus the model's.

### Cited Findings
- Harville (1980): market correctly picked 72% of winners vs 70% for his statistical procedure. — [Stekler et al. review](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2009-002.pdf)
- Glickman & Stern (1998), JASA, Bayesian state-space model (team strengths evolving week to week): predictions for the last eight weeks of the 1993 season were "comparable to the betting line" (not better). — [Stekler et al. review](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2009-002.pdf); [SIAM chapter listing](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1.9780898718386.ch5)
- Zuber et al. (1985) / Sauer et al. (1988): a model picked profitable sides 59% in 1983 but only 39% in 1984 — classic non-persistence. — [Stekler et al. review](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2009-002.pdf)
- Boulier & Stekler (2003), IJF: power-score forecasts 61% winners vs market 65%. — [Stekler et al. review](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2009-002.pdf)
- Song, Boulier & Stekler (2007), IJF: ~31,000 forecasts, 2000-2001 seasons, 70 experts and 32 statistical systems. Systems 62% and experts 62% on winners vs market 66%; ATS, both systems and experts were 50% ("the naive model of flipping a coin"). "No evidence that either statistical systems or experts consistently outperform the market." — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v23y2007i3p405-413.html); [GWU WP 2007-001](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2007-001.pdf)
- Baker & McHale (2013), IJF: point-process model of NFL scoring with hazards adjusted by team stats and betting-market indicators; for exact scores it did "at least as well as the market," but for game outcomes it was "marginally outperformed by the betting market" (evaluated including Kelly betting). — [Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1432547/forecasting-exact-scores-in-national-football-league-games)
- Warner (2010, Cornell project; not peer-reviewed): Gaussian-process model, ~2,000 games 2000-2009; 64.36% SU winners but below 52.4% ATS breakeven. — [ML betting review, arXiv 2410.21484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21484)
- Patel (2023, as summarized in review): Elastic Net/RF/XGBoost on 2016-2021; 53.65% on the 2021 test season — one season, ~270 games, not significant. Juuri et al. (2023) reported 95.61% SVM accuracy (implausible; likely leakage) with no ROI. — [arXiv 2410.21484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21484)
- Hubáček & Šír, "Beating the market with a bad predictive model" (IJF 2023): profit requires reducing partial correlation Corr[T, M | R] between the trader's and market's estimates given truth; for Kelly betting, wealth growth W_G = D_KL(R||M) − D_KL(R||T), i.e., profit depends on relative accuracy and decorrelation; taker selectivity provides structural advantage. — [arXiv 2010.12508](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12508); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.Org/a/eee/intfor/v39y2023i2p691-719.html)
- Hubáček, Šourek & Železný (2019), IJF, "Exploiting sports-betting market using machine learning" (NBA): introduced decorrelation from bookmaker as an explicit training objective. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v35y2019i2p783-796.html); [CTU page](https://ida.fel.cvut.cz/papers/hubacek2019exploiting.html)
- Walsh & Joshi (2024; cited in review, NBA): calibration-optimized model selection produced 69.86% higher average returns than accuracy-optimized selection. — [arXiv 2410.21484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21484)
- Wunderlich & Memmert (2020), IJF: positive betting returns can occur without superior accuracy; betting returns are not a valid accuracy measure and must be assessed alongside accuracy, in- and out-of-sample. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v36y2020i2p713-722.html)

### Inferences
- The project's blend result (blend 0.609 worse than Vegas 0.607) means the model carries essentially no information orthogonal to the closing price. Per Hubáček & Šír, that is the binding constraint: adding features that correlate with what the market already prices (team strength, EPA, QB quality) cannot help; only features whose errors are decorrelated from the market's could.
- Applying their Kelly identity to the project's numbers: with model log loss 0.621 vs market 0.607, expected log-growth from Kelly-betting the model's probabilities against a vig-free close is roughly −0.014 nats per game (negative), before vig. This is a direct theoretical confirmation of the observed outcome (my calculation, using the paper's formula).
- No peer-reviewed NFL paper using player-level (RAPM-style) or modern ML models demonstrates out-of-sample profit against closing lines.

### Gaps
- I did not find a peer-reviewed NFL study of Bayesian hierarchical or player-based models evaluated against closing lines after 2013.
- Full-text Glickman & Stern MSE comparison numbers were not retrievable (SIAM blocked).

## 4. How much information the market incorporates, and when (injuries, weather, betting splits)

### Takeaway
The market incorporates public bettor sentiment into openers-to-close moves (sometimes overreacting), does not follow a balanced-book model, and there is some peer-reviewed evidence weather (humidity, wind) is under-priced in totals. Betting-split (public %) contrarian strategies show no excess returns. I found no rigorous academic study of NFL injury/QB news incorporation timing.

### Cited Findings
- Betting splits: 2005-2009, 1,278 games, four offshore books — public money skewed to favorites/road favorites/streaking teams, but fading the public produced no significant excess returns. — [Paul, Weinbach & Humphreys WP](https://sites.ualberta.ca/~econwps/2011/wp2011-16.pdf)
- Sportsbooks don't balance books; bettor biases toward best teams. — [Humphreys, Paul & Weinbach 2013](https://fitpublishing.com/node/1071); [Levitt 2004](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/users/aldous/157/Papers/Levitt_Gambling_2004.pdf)
- Weather: humidity significantly explains actual-minus-total; humidity/wind strategies reject efficiency. — [FIT Publishing](https://fitpublishing.com/node/3503)
- Sentiment-driven line moves: predictable from pre-opening variables. — [Avery & Chevalier 1999](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jnlbus/v72y1999i4p493-521.html); momentum overreaction then reversal — [Moskowitz](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f92072/f92072.pdf)

### Inferences
- The project's null result on weather, matchups, and referees vs the close is consistent with these signals being priced by close in the modern market (the weather paper's data period is unspecified and may predate widespread modern weather pricing).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study quantifying NFL injury/QB-news underreaction or the timing of injury-news incorporation was found; search results were exclusively media/industry guides.
- No study on modern (post-2018 legalization) market microstructure (limits, sharp vs. recreational books) for NFL was found in the academic literature.

## 5. How accurate must a model be to profit?

### Takeaway
At -110 pricing you need 52.38% ATS; given an SD of ~13.6 points for (margin − line), that requires a true expected edge of roughly 0.8 points per bet vs the line you take. Experts and 32 statistical systems in 2000-2001 hit only 50% ATS; nfelo's MAE matches the close only to within 0.01 points. The theoretical condition is relative KL divergence (log loss) plus decorrelation, not raw accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- Breakeven 52.38% at standard vig; SD of line error ≈ 13.59 points (2002-2011). — [arXiv 1211.4000](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.4000)
- Kelly growth = D_KL(R||M) − D_KL(R||T); a model must have lower expected log loss than the market on the bets it takes, or be decorrelated so that its selected bets have that property. — [arXiv 2010.12508](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12508)
- ATS accuracy of 32 systems and 70 experts = 50% (2000-2001). — [GWU WP 2007-001](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2007-001.pdf)
- Model MAE equal to closing line (+0.01) still produced positive claimed ATS results in a vendor backtest — showing that MAE parity can coexist with apparent profit via selection, but not peer-reviewed. — [nfelo](https://nfeloapp.com/games/nfl-model-performance/)
- Calibration matters more than accuracy for returns. — [arXiv 2410.21484 (Walsh & Joshi)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21484); betting return not a valid accuracy measure — [Wunderlich & Memmert 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v36y2020i2p713-722.html)

### Inferences
- Rough edge requirement (my calculation): Φ⁻¹(0.5238) ≈ 0.060 SD; × 13.6 ≈ 0.81 points of true mean edge per bet needed at -110. A model whose overall MAE is equal to the close has zero average edge; it can only profit if its *disagreements* with the close are right more often than chance, which is what CLV measures.
- Sample-size implication: detecting a 54% true rate vs 52.38% at 95% confidence needs roughly 3,700+ bets — more than a decade of all NFL games — which is why most published anomalies cannot be confirmed or refuted on one era.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper explicitly tabulating the log-loss or Brier gap vs NFL closing lines required for profitability was found.

## 6. College football market efficiency

### Takeaway
CFB evidence shows more persistent biases than the NFL (favorites overpriced, home teams underpriced, holdover overvaluation of last year's top 10, momentum mispricing), but follow-ups show efficiency improving over time (coach-based edge disappeared in the last 5 years of the sample; totals overs bias corrected by 2003-2015).

### Cited Findings
- Sinkey & Logan (2014), Eastern Economic Journal: >14,000 CFB games 1985-2008; favorites statistically overpriced, home teams underpriced; books shade "hot" teams, and tempering the hot hand yields profitable simple strategies. Working-paper version (1985-2003, >11,000 games): favorites 1.86-6.84% less likely to cover; betting $1,000 against favorites returns ~$1,017 before transaction costs (i.e., not net-profitable at -110). — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/easeco/v40y2014i4p583-603.html); [AEA 2010 WP](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2010/retrieve.php?pdfid=406)
- Bennett (2019), Atlantic Economic Journal: first game of the season, prior season AP top-10 teams overvalued; bets against them won significantly more than 52.4%, especially vs non-Power 5 opponents; ranks 11-25 showed no inefficiency. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/atlecj/v47y2019i1d10.1007_s11293-019-09611-y.html)
- Cox, Schwartz, Van Ness & Van Ness (2021), Journal of Sports Economics: CFB spreads more predictive in regular season than bowls; market efficient on favorites covering, but mispricing of (especially negative) momentum within transaction costs. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/jospec/v22y2021i3p251-273.html)
- Francisco & Moore (2018), Journal of Economics and Finance: revisiting Paul & Weinbach (2005)'s CFB totals "over overvalued" result with 2003-2015 data — "the market has largely corrected itself"; neither always-over nor always-under is profitable. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jecfin/v42y2018i4d10.1007_s12197-018-9437-y.html)
- Farinella & Moffett (Business Quest, Univ. of West Georgia online journal, marked "Peer Reviewed"; data 2004-2013, posted in the 2016 volume): highest-paid coaches, 1,937 games; 53.95% ATS over 10 years (rejects fair bet but not profitable net of vig); last 5 years 51.6% (no edge); no totals strategy profitable; efficiency improving over time. — [westga.edu PDF](https://www.westga.edu/~bquest/2016/football2016.pdf)

### Inferences
- CFB's larger team count, wider talent gaps, and less-bet games (smaller conferences, early season) are where the literature finds the most persistent mispricing; early-season holdover bias vs non-P5 opponents is the most specific, recent (2019) peer-reviewed lead.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study specifically on Group of Five/FCS/small-conference CFB line efficiency or on CFB opener vs closer accuracy was found.
- No CFB study testing modern ratings models (SP+, FPI-style) against closing lines was found.
